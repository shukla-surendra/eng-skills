## A new lease of life

(Repeat) Renewed vitality or opportunity.

A renewed sense of energy or purpose.

## Abide by

To follow rules or laws.

## Abrupt

**Meaning:** Sudden or unexpected, often in a way that seems rude.  
 **Example:** His abrupt departure surprised everyone.  
 **Context:** Behavior, actions.

## Abstract & Descriptive Adjectives (sinful, surreal, compelling...)


## Account

**Meaning:** A report or description of an event or experience.  
**Example:** The witness gave a detailed account of the accident.  
**Context:** Reporting, description.

## Act upon

To take action based on something.

## Action point

Specific task assigned to be done.

## Aim

**Meaning:** To have a goal or purpose.  
 **Example:** Our aim is to improve customer satisfaction.  
 **Context:** Goals, objectives.

## All over

Everywhere; also used to mean something is finished.

## All spheres of life

All areas or aspects of life (e.g., personal, professional, social).

## All the way

Completely; to the fullest extent.

## Ample

**Meaning:** More than enough; plentiful.  
 **Example:** There was ample space for all the guests.  
 **Context:** Quantity, space.

## Appealing

**Meaning:** Attractive or interesting.  
 **Example:** The idea of a holiday by the beach is very appealing.  
 **Context:** Opinions, preferences.

## As far as sb is concerned

**Meaning:** Regarding someone’s opinion or perspective.  
 **Example:** As far as I’m concerned, the meeting is unnecessary.  
 **Context:** Opinions, views.

## As of yet

Until now; so far (usually with something pending).

## Assess

**Meaning:** To evaluate or judge.  
 **Example:** The teacher will assess your performance.  
 **Context:** Evaluation.

## Back out of

To withdraw from an agreement.

## Back to square one

**Meaning:** To start over.  
 **Example:** The deal fell through, so it’s back to square one.  
 **Context:** Restart.

## Back up

To support; or to make a data copy; or reverse a vehicle.

## Bare metal

Refers to physical hardware without software layers; often used in IT or cloud computing.

## Be just the thing

Be exactly what’s needed or perfect for the situation.

## Be noticeable

To stand out or be visible.

## Beat

**Meaning:** To defeat someone; also to strike repeatedly.  
 **Example:** Our team beat theirs in the finals.  
 **Context:** Sports, competition.

## Beat the shit outta you

(Profane) To violently attack someone; also used metaphorically.

## Beating around the bush

Avoiding the main topic or issue; speaking indirectly.

## Begin to do something

To start an action.

## Below the belt

Unfair or personally hurtful, often in arguments.

## Bend over backward

To make a great effort to help or accommodate someone.

## Bend the knee

To submit or show deference, especially to authority.

## Bit off more than I can chew

**Meaning:** To take on more responsibility than one can manage.  
 **Example:** I bit off more than I can chew with this project.  
 **Context:** Overcommitment.

## Blend them together

Combine two or more elements smoothly.

## Blink of an eye

A very brief moment; happens extremely fast.

## Blow away

**Meaning:** To impress strongly or be very surprised.  
**Example:** The performance blew me away.  
**Context:** Impressed.

## Blow out

To extinguish; also means to defeat easily in sports.

## Bolt-on

An additional component that integrates into an existing system (used in business/tech).

## Borrow

**Meaning:** To take something temporarily.  
 **Example:** Can I borrow your pen?  
 **Context:** Lending, temporary use.

## Bowl over

**Meaning:** To surprise or impress someone greatly.  
**Example:** His kindness bowled me over.  
**Context:** Surprise.

## Branch out

To expand into new areas or markets.

## Break down

To stop functioning; emotionally collapse; or analyze into smaller parts.

Repeated: To stop working, collapse, or analyze.

Repeated: To stop working or decompose.

## Break into

To enter by force; or start doing something suddenly.

## Break into (vs. break through)

**Break into**: Enter by force (a building); start doing something suddenly.  
 **Break through**: Forcefully make a way through a barrier or difficulty.

## Break off

To end suddenly (talks, engagement).

## Break out

To escape or start suddenly (war, fire, disease).

## Break up

To end a relationship; or disperse.

## Break with

To end association or tradition.

## Bring about

To cause something to happen.

## Bring in

To introduce (new law, idea, person).

## Bring up

To mention; or to raise a child.

## Bump into

To meet someone by chance.

## Call off

To cancel something.

Repeated: To cancel.

## Call on

To request someone to speak or act.

## Call out/in

To publicly criticize / to summon for duty.

## Call upon

To formally ask someone to do something.

## Carry on

Repeated: To continue.

## Carry out

To perform or execute a task.

## Cash in

To convert to money; to take advantage of something.

## Cast off

To discard or get rid of.

## Catch up on

To make up for lost progress.

## Catfish

To deceive someone online with a fake identity (esp. romantically).

## Character Traits & Descriptions (defiant, enduring, extravagant...)


## Charity begins at home

Take care of your own family and responsibilities before helping others.

## Check in

To register at a hotel or airport; or touch base.

## Cherry-pick

Selectively choose the best or most convenient items.

## Chime in

To interrupt or join a conversation with a comment.

## Chip in

To contribute money or effort collectively.

## Chipping away / Chip away

Gradually reduce or weaken something (e.g., "chipping away at a problem").

## Choke back

**Meaning:** To hold back tears or emotions.  
**Example:** She choked back her tears during the speech.  
**Context:** Emotions.

## Chummy

**Meaning:** Friendly, sociable.  
 **Example:** They are very chummy with each other.  
 **Context:** Relationships.

## Circle back

Return to a topic or person later.

## Clap back

To respond sharply or insultingly to criticism.

## Clean up

**Meaning:** To tidy or make a place clean; also, to fix or improve a situation.  
**Example:** We need to clean up this mess before the guests arrive.  
**Context:** Housekeeping, fixing problems.

## Clickbait

Misleading or sensational content meant to attract clicks.

## Cling (on) to

**Meaning:** To hold tightly to something or someone.  
**Example:** The child clung to his mother.  
**Context:** Physical or emotional attachment.

## Close down

To permanently shut a business.

## Clouding judgment

Obstructing or distorting clear thinking or decision-making.

## Come across

To appear a certain way; or to find unexpectedly.

## Come by

To obtain something; or visit briefly.

## Come down

To decline, fall, or decrease.

## Come from

To originate in place, background, or context.

## Come on over

**Meaning:** To invite someone to visit.  
 **Example:** Why don’t you come on over this evening?  
 **Context:** Invitation.

## Come through / Came through

**Meaning:** To succeed or survive a difficult situation.  
 **Example:** He came through the surgery successfully.  
 **Context:** Survival, success.

## Come to

To regain consciousness; also used for arriving at decisions or conclusions.

## Come to grief

Experience failure, hardship, or injury.

## Come up

To arise or occur unexpectedly.

## Come up with

To think of (an idea, plan, solution, etc.).

To invent or produce an idea or solution.

Repeated: To think of an idea or plan.

## Compelling

**Meaning:** Very interesting or convincing.  
 **Example:** She gave a compelling argument.  
 **Context:** Speech, writing.

## Course correct

To adjust your strategy or direction to get back on track.

## Crack up

**Meaning:** To laugh uncontrollably or to become mentally ill.  
**Example:** The comedian cracked us up.  
**Context:** Humor, mental health.

## Cry Loudly (Weeping) vs. Cry Out

**Cry loudly** = weep intensely; **Cry out** = yell or scream, usually briefly.

## Cry out / Cry loud

**Cry out**: Shout loudly due to pain, fear, or urgency.  
 **Cry loud**: Can mean shouting or weeping loudly, context matters.

## Cut back

To reduce (expenses, activities).

## Cut down

Repeated: To reduce.

## Cut down on

To reduce something (like expenses, sugar).

## Cut loose

**Meaning:** To relax and enjoy oneself freely.  
 **Example:** It’s time to cut loose and have fun!  
 **Context:** Relaxation.

## Cut off

To disconnect (call, utilities); or isolate.

Repeated: To stop supply or communication.

## Cut to the chase

**Meaning:** To get to the point without wasting time.  
 **Example:** Let’s cut to the chase and discuss the budget.  
 **Context:** Efficiency.

## Day tripper

Someone who goes on short trips and returns the same day.

## Dazzle me
